## How we work, since 19 November

- **One builder a piece, one gate.** Each piece is built by one builder on a branch of its own (`night/g-...`). It reaches `main` only through one automatic gate, the landing tool, which merges the branch in a scratch checkout, runs the core's tests with both golden tests, a sweep of eight islands and the pictures, and only then moves `main` and pushes it. Nothing reaches `main` another way. The pictures are taken and looked at; they are no longer compared pixel by pixel.
- **Chains and batches.** A line of stages that each build on the one before (the run to 2050's, the sandbox's) is built as a chain: each stage's builder starts from the branch of the stage before, and the chain reports when its last stage is done. Pieces that are ready together are merged onto one branch as a batch, where they are mended where they meet (the switch lists, the Rules tab's walk, the game's golden hashes), and the batch goes through the gate once. Two batches landed on 19 November.
- **The faults' round.** What a builder sees and leaves goes, a line a fault, to [backlog/for-the-bugfix-round.md](backlog/for-the-bugfix-round.md), and a round of mending takes them up together when the chains in flight have landed (the first step back, below, says what its first batch holds).
- **What is mended at once:** anything that breaks the build or the tests on `main`, unbalances the books, makes a run fail, or stops the game being played. Every other fault is written down in one line and waits for a later round of mending. The landing tool files each piece's faults in [backlog/for-the-bugfix-round.md](backlog/for-the-bugfix-round.md) as it lands.
- **The big features first.** The features that may be decisive come first (The order of work, below). Whether a piece works is judged by measuring the island, not by reading its code, and each builder reports the numbers that show it.
- **The working build** (since 26 November, b0426ac). A builder builds the core under the `work` profile and runs `run_tests.py --profile work --quick` while working, and the whole set once before handing over; the viewer stays in the dev profile. The release profile, the gate and the golden hashes are as they were.
- **A step back, every now and then:** a review of the design, the architecture, speed, the goals and how we work, between pieces. The first was taken on 19 November and the second on 20 November (both below).
- **The simple core** (her note of 19 November, at the foot): which rules the island still needs, found by turning each off. `isostate-core ablate` is its tool and [core.md](core.md) the page it writes. It is run again at each step back and after a week of new rules. A rule found idle there is then switched off under the game's rules and kept, with a line in its page saying since when and why; two were, on 20 November (The order of work, 4).
- **The managing session** holds the plan and the decisions and stays out of the details. The earlier pages call it the director.

## How it is kept

- **A note goes in here first**, dated and word for word, in "The notes, as written" at the foot. It is not started because it was said.
- **A note typed in the game is a note like any other.** Since 26 November N, anywhere in the game, opens a line over it; Enter keeps her words with where she stood (the island, the date on the clock, the camera, the town or square chosen, the panel and map view open, the session, the build) and a picture of the screen, in `notes/` in the saved games' folder, beside the sessions (the viewer's README, "A note in your words"). The managing session reads that folder and files the words at the foot, word for word, with the place they were typed in.
- **The help card shows this file** (The backlog and your notes of today): it reads In flight, the headings of the order of work and Waiting on Stella from here, so those three keep their shape: a table under In flight, a `###` heading for each line of the order of work, and a bold lead for each item waiting on her.
- **Notes on one thing are gathered** and taken up together, so that a second thought can change the first before any work is done.
- **The backlog is gone through by the managing session** after landings (whenever a piece is merged into `main`) and when a round of notes arrives; the faults a landing brings are already filed by the landing tool (above). Notes the landed work has answered are closed, with where. Notes it has made moot are pruned, with a line saying why. The rest are ranked again.
- **A note is taken up when its time has come:** nothing in flight is rewriting the same code or the same part of the picture; what it depends on has landed; and it ranks above whatever else is ready. Two exceptions: what is mended at once (How we work, above), and a fix of a line or two in code nobody is working in.
- **The ranking**, highest first: what breaks play or makes the picture say something untrue; what many other things wait on; what she has raised more than once; what makes the island's economics truer; polish.
- **What helpers and reviewers find** along the way is kept on a page of its own, [backlog/found-along-the-way.md](backlog/found-along-the-way.md), ranked in the same list as her notes, and never dressed as her note. ## The second step back, 20 November

Four analysts, one of them followed by a checker, took four questions to `main` as it stood on the morning of 20 November: who loses when the machines come, why the island has no city, why the century after 1970 is poor, and where the code hurts. What they found:

- **Who loses is not yet played.** The haulers' share of all hands halves from 1900 to 1950 (0.356 to 0.182), the clerks' rises to 0.167 by 1970 and falls back to 0.099 by 2050, and no machine tends in any works in any decade. Had each class its own pay, haulers would earn about half the mean in 1950 and clerks under two thirds in 2050; but every such figure was reckoned afterwards from the shares the island played, with how fast hands follow the work set by hand, and no pay rule was played.
- **No town was a city.** In 2050 the largest town held about 70,000 townsfolk, a ninth of all townsfolk, where a small country's first city holds a fifth to a half; none of seventeen arms tried from 1900 made one. Income a head was level across the towns to within three or four per cent, so people moving had nothing to chase; and demand never changed its shape, services holding 0.28 of hands from 1900 to 2050.
- **The late century's depressions were a fault of the measure.** The falls of 20 to 33 per cent after 1970 were the price of timber, which swings fivefold every four or five years, read through the 1750 basket. Valued at fixed prices, what was made fell at most 7 per cent after 1990: 4 depressions on sixteen islands where the old measure counted 65. A measure chained every year drifts as well.
- **Growth after 1960 is new things being found, and few are.** The methods stand at their caps, and offices and office machines are all that come after 1960. Coal that never runs out, learning that goes on and the land machines' ceiling rising on would each raise 2050 over 1970 (1.41, 1.48 and 1.38, all three 1.97, against 1.22), but each sets a level, not a trend: about a point a year of the slowdown is still unexplained.
- **Sweeps queued on one heap.** Islands played on many threads of one process waited on the process's one heap of memory: 24 at once kept about five of 48 processors busy, and the four hours of the core's table were mostly that wait.

## One cost of ground (her notes of 27 November)

Her notes are at the foot. Nothing is built for them and nothing is started. What is said to be so was read on `main` the same day.

- **What she asked:** that roads, rails, rivers, and the boundaries of cities and buildings share one system underneath. The land has a construction cost from its grade and its substrate, each thing to be built has its own method of grade against cost, and terraforming and landscaping come in ("a thing I think we need"). Then: knocking down what stands in the way as a cost of its own; owners who cannot always be made to sell; an upgraded road keeping the cuts already made in the land.
- **What is so:** the same ground is priced five ways. Railways and canals share a card on the 2 km cells (`ways::BuildCost`: lowland, hills, mountains, a river, a slope, a cell already laid). Roads' courses are sought over the 125 m squares with prices of their own (`courses.rs`: a metre climbed is worth so many metres of level road, a ruling gradient by the kind of road, wet ground). Crossings price a ford by the metre of water and build a bridge once (`crossings.rs`). Building ground has a score for steep and wet squares that orders which are taken, and nobody pays it (`squares::ground_cost`). Carting prices the use of a way and not its building. The land holds heights in metres, wet ground, rivers and seven rocks. Nothing moves earth, and no ground is ever changed by work.
- **Proposed by Claude, and liked ("Yeah, I like it a lot"):** one price of earth (the hands and goods it takes to move a cubic metre of soil, rock or marsh); a card for each thing built (its footprint, the grade it bears, the structures it may use: bridge, tunnel, lock, retaining wall, piles); one finder for strips (roads, rails, canals) and one for plots (houses, works, fields). Levelling a plot on a slope costs with the cube of its side, so a works that needs a wide level floor seeks the valley and cottages climb the hill in terraces. Three additions:
  - A course is chosen by what it costs to build and what it costs to haul over it, for the traffic expected. A lane then follows the ground and a trunk road cuts through it, by one rule.
  - Digging is a task with machines (shovel and barrow, the steam shovel, the bulldozer), so that the same hill is a wall in 1760 and a cutting in 1960. Canals that wind along the contours, then graded railways, then motorways should come out of the years.
  - Earth moved stays moved. An upgrade pays only for the extra earth, old lines are kept, cuttings and embankments show in the picture, and the sandbox's tool for shaping land is the same system with the bill off.
- **From her additions:** what stands on the ground is part of the cost, to knock down and to buy. Going round a town may then beat going through it, and a station stop at the town's edge, which could take the place of the rule that begins bypasses in 1920 (the clean-out's note 3). Owners who refuse, or ask more than the worth, come late, with compulsory purchase as an act of the political mode.
- **Kept apart, for later (her second note):**
  - A town or a works hemming in its river to gain ground: an embankment paid by the rent of the ground made dry.
  - Meanders that answer to the bank's material. Today a reach wanders by its flow, its fall and the width of its valley floor (`landwater.rs`), and the rock has no part. A change makes every island's land anew, so it waits for a planned move.
  - Foundations by the ground and the building's weight. The generator reckons the depth of soft ground and the land does not keep it.
  - Fertility that work improves (drainage, terraces). Today it is read from the rock, the slope, the rain and the river's silt, and marsh takes six tenths of it away.
  - Irrigation. It matters little on islands with 900 mm of rain a year, where silt already draws farms to the rivers, and it would decide everything on a dry one. Proposed beside it: climate as a setting of the island.
  - Her map note 7 (watersheds, floods, dams, reservoirs, irrigation) is the same thread.
- **The order:** a design study first, as for leaving the grid. Then in stages, each built switched off and turned on in a planned move, since what gets built and when changes on every island: the ground's price as a map view; roads on it; earth kept; railways and canals; plots; digging as a task; shaping land, and water that answers. Roads are the first user. The mend of the road faults of 27 November (hard corners where roads meet, roads side by side on hills, short zigzags, villages beside their roads) keeps its fixes of the look (a road runs on through a place, a village is laid out from its crossroads, the works' clearance reads the courses) and gives up its invented numbers for the finder on the ground's price.
