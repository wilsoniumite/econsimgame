## What breaks play, or makes the picture say something untrue

Two copies of the game on one island write the same autosave and the later one wins (L121); for a moment while a new game sets the old one aside there is no latest save at all (L126). `--load` with no file after it takes the next flag for the file (C138); a save doctored to the year 5000 is accepted and played for 3,251 years (C139); nothing bounds the number of families a save or `--families` may name (L127). Never run through the real program: the refusal of a bad `--save` place and the `.unread` file, the card shown when an earlier save is used, and a whole century saved and loaded on the merged build (L128, L124, L122). And small autosaves dated 11 November in `%APPDATA%\Isolated State\saves` may be a helper's and not hers (L49). *Why:* the one piece here that can cost Stella a game she is playing. Hours, not days. *Trigger:* after `night/calendar` lands, which changes the save's layout, `saves.rs` and `live.rs`. *Reported:* the saved-games builder and reviewer, the viewer's integrator, the far view's housekeeping.

3. **Carts, roads and bridges: what helpers found, for Ready next 2** (notes 19 and 20). ## What she has raised more than once

15. **Carting and roads that cost, for Ready next 7** (notes 20 and 21). What the rules pages add to "nobody pays". A road becomes a turnpike by itself when the traffic passes a threshold, and then costs what a navigable river costs, 0.3 a cell (C113). The slope penalty is charged by the origin cell's road alone, so the same journey costs differently each way (L216). The graph has one cost for all goods, and bulk gains only through weight (C114). All of the carting bill is counted as men's time: horses, fodder, wagons and tolls are not in the model, and an eighth of all hands are on the road in 1800 (R12). The freight rate is fixed in money while the money wage rises, so by 1900 a cartload takes two fifths fewer hands with no better road; the carting page left this for a decision (R14, C91). Carters are taken from the town that is paid the carting and not from along the road, a month late through a damper, and a new quarry or colliery town can have three hands in ten carting (R17, R18, R19). *Trigger:* as Ready next 7. *Reported:* `docs/rules/carting.md` and `people.md`, the rail probe, the railways reviewer, the map of the core.

16. **A railway company, for Ready next 7** (note 21, who pays and who is paid). Subscribers get nothing back: in company the fares come to 23 to 26 per cent of the capital a year and nobody owns them (R86). Calls on subscribers have no limit and nothing abandons a line: the worst line of a run costs 3.0 times its estimate on average and up to 4.6, the slowest takes six and a half years, and canals call on their subscribers the same way (R87, R88, C82, K15). A line takes 24 months whatever its length and buys everything at one town's prices (L215). A refused district keeps promoters away from a town within four cells for five years (C94). The honest repair is a company with fares and shares, with the canals' promoters as the pattern. *Trigger:* as Ready next 7, and the books of account. *Reported:* `docs/rules/railways.md`, the rail probe, the railways reviewer, `railways.rs` itself.

## What makes the island's economics truer

18. **What the pass over all the rules has to settle, for Ready next 5.** It comes first in this rank because pieces 19 and 20 wait on its scorecard. Fifty-one rows, which are thirty-two faults:
    - *Growth comes early and stops.* Output per head peaks in 1883 on average and then falls; the cause is unproven and nobody ran the counterfactual (R121, C36, C100, C123). Machine quality sits at its cap of 4 by 1900 (R56, C16). The converter is found between 1816 and 1830 on three seeds of six, and its numbers were never tuned again with the works life cycle (R57, C15). An engine works does not pay for thirty years after it is known (R107). Farms are never all mechanised, 0.83 in 1900 (R62).
    - *The wage.* It dipped when kept town ground came in, 2.59 for 2.80 in 1900. The likely cause is town ground taken whole from the plough, whose mend the towns page left for a session of its own (R99, C128). The late wage with families is 3 to 6 per cent lower under the people rules (R68).
    - *Swings.* A building cycle nobody chose, with timber trebling every three or four years (R111). The year's room throttles a trade far too small for its market: tools' dearest year is 5.8 times their median, and three cures were tried and none kept (R112, C27). Prices swing harder under the life cycle, most with families (R113, C39, R79, C7). After a loss of capacity the rebuilding overshoots by 18 per cent and rings for twenty years (R120). Works run emptier, 0.78 for the port's 0.84 (R116, C132). Alarms of downturn are 17.5 a run for the port's 15 (C124). Sister works are still about a quarter of all works (R117).
    - *Famines.* 2.2 years a run; the Engel curve about doubles the notices (R38, C49, C130).
    - *The steel rail* is a fifth of rails in 1890 and never built on one island of eight (R58, C17, C129).
    - *The railway age* runs from no line at all to 1,936 km in 1850, and its date wanders with the discovery (R89, C89, C135, R16). The promoters' hurdle and the induced traffic are still the port's numbers, and the stricter-promoter experiment was never run again on the repaired build (R94, C83).
    - *The towns' share* reaches 0.41 to 0.43 by 1900, short of the plan's 0.5 (R36).
    - *Numbers never searched, or set on a few seeds:* the people rule's (R69, R77); kept ground's (R103); the life cycle's five (R119, C34); `adopt_span` and `quality_b` (C20); Engel's strength, ceiling and `y_ref`, which was set beside a 1750 income of 1.84 to 1.89 that is now 1.48 (C50, R39, C54); the harvest's lesser numbers (C68); the merchants' markup, not swept since carting, people or towns (C3); and the plan's targets for 1750 to 1800, set without carting (R13, C92).

    *Trigger:* as Ready next 5, and after pieces 11 and 12. *Reported:* every rules page's "what worries me", the reviewers and integrators of each mechanism, the merged-game sweeps.

19. **New towns, and districts that change market.** A new town's first year is reckoned at its donor's prices whole, so Fenmere's 445 town people are billed 0.98 of their wages in rent that year (R98). The founding test counts the district's carting saving and not the island's, and late towns cost the wage 3 to 6 per cent (R70, C106). When a district changes market its prices, expectations and wage are set by a rule that leans on the port's carting and handling charges (R71), and a new town pays more for what it must buy in (R72). Markets side by side are unstable: a railway can move a district between markets in a year and a town's people jump, and canals will do the same (R73). The game has 13.5 towns in 1800 for the port's 18.9, and one island ends with 14 (C127). A harbour town on a spit can run out of neighbours to grow onto, which the new land's shores make likelier (R105). There is no rule for railway towns; builder and reviewer both declined to invent one the prices do not speak for (R74). Several days. *Trigger:* after canals are merged and the scorecard exists. *Reported:* `docs/rules/people.md` and `towns.md`, the growth probe, the merged-game sweeps.

20. **A harvest month** (bears on note 10). The island has no seasons: a bad year is twelve short months, with no harvest month and no stocks that mean anything (R48, C69). Grain's price saws for more than a year after a shock (R46) and answers a short harvest too weakly: ten per cent less grain is nine per cent dearer, where in the eighteenth century it was more like thirty (R49). The country's harvest may vary more late in the century; three seeds, one look (R47). Families and the landlord bot rank land by last year's rent as paid, so they chase wherever the harvest was best and pass over a district's poorest cells for a year, and nobody has looked for a family ruined by one harvest (R50, R51, R197, C67). Several days. *Trigger:* after `night/calendar` lands and the scorecard exists. *Reported:* `docs/rules/harvest.md`, the harvest reviewer.

21. **What the tree's later stages inherit, with Ready next 8 and 9.** No foreign trade, which is the tree's third stage (R161); cotton against wool and the world's prices are guesses, wrong the first time (R183). Corn mills' costs are nine tenths grain (R184); fishing and gardens' yields and the stream's capacity are first guesses, which bears on note 7 (R185); the shipyards are never found until fleets are built (R188). Steel still has no use of its own in the engine (R60), and a steel rail's longer life is credited at the mill, so it cheapens new track as well as relaying (R59, C22). Improvements as data need more than two machine slots a sector (R190). The tree's thresholds scale with the island, so a larger or richer generator brings every new good forward: check at the new land's landing (R181). For logging, note 8: woods and pasture get nothing that raises output per worker, and woods fall from 4.4 to 2.8 a worker-month (C105); a tree has no age, so every tree in a wood is drawn full grown (K33). *Trigger:* after `night/tree` lands. *Reported:* the tree's design and its critique, `docs/rules/machines.md`, the growth probe.
