## Needs a person at the window

No helper can judge these: each is how the game feels when it is played or watched at a real screen.

- *The sandbox, played at a window.* Added 19 November. Since that morning `--sandbox` opens a game nobody plays a family in, with a tray of tools under the clock (Look, Money, People, Harvest) and Run on for 1, 10 or 25 years (the viewer's README; [designs/sandbox.md](../designs/sandbox.md), section 6.1). Every picture of it so far was taken with no window, by a script; nobody has picked up a tool, clicked a town or used Run on at a real screen. She asked for it to make testing easier for her and because it may be fun in itself, and only she can say whether it is either.
- *The new island, played at a window.* The land made by water is the game's since the evening of 19 November (`docs/rules/land.md`, "The switch"; the viewer's README, "The new land"): every new game is played on it and needs no flag, `--old-land` starts one on the old island, and a game saved before is played on the old island it was saved on. It has the ground, rivers, lakes and sea from the core's heights, each cell's meeting place on dry ground, railways that find their way by the ground, gentler rivers, and roads that keep to one bank and cross on a bridge, a ford or a ferry where the simulation has one. Every picture of it so far was taken with no window; nobody has played it, panned over it or watched a year turn on it at a real screen, and her notes on the game wait until she has seen it (her note 24).
- *The smooth year's turn.* Its second stage landed on 19 November (0c4490e; the viewer's README): a year's trees, houses and works go to the screen in pieces of 6 MB, at most 13 MB a frame, each kind changing in one frame, and the year's arrival is off the main thread. With no window, in fourteen seconds at Fast from 1750, frames over 33 ms fell from 37 to none on the new land; the price is that a year takes about a second and a half to go out. Nobody has watched it at a screen: whether a year's change arriving a second and a half late reads as calm or as lag, and whether anything still stops at a year's end. (The first stage's questions, about a half second of new works among last year's trees and years shown one time in two and a half, were overtaken by it; `docs/probes/dx-smooth.md`, section 12, has them.)
- *The pay chart and its two dials.* Added 20 November (`docs/rules/pay.md`). In a sandbox the Work half of the Markets panel opens on "Pay by class of task, reckoned and not played": three charts from 1900 (each class's pay over the mean; each class's real pay against 1900; the schooled over the plain), with two dials over them in plain words, how sharply pay bends to the work wanted (0.1, 0.3, 1, 2) and how many years hands take to follow the work (5, 10, 20, 30), also pinned at the head of the Rules tab. Moving a chip redraws the charts with nothing replayed. At 0.3 and ten years a clerk's real pay in 2050 is 0.86 of 1970's while the wage rises 1.20-fold; at 2 and 30 nobody loses outright. Whether the dials read as she would put the question, and whether the charts say what her research would ask of them, only she can say.
- *The first city.* Added 20 November (`docs/rules/city.md`). Run on to 2050 from a plain start, the largest town holds about 130,000 townsfolk on the mean island, a fifth of all townsfolk; on island 7 it is Dunheath, 174,000. The picture draws it as an even carpet of two-storey houses and works' yards with a church and a gasworks at its middle, since nothing taller is drawn yet (the city's page, "What the picture gets wrong for a town of that size"). Worth her eye before the look by era is built: whether a city of that size, so drawn, reads as a city at all, and where she would want the look to begin.
- *The clock as it plays.* A pause late in a year moves the clock on to the next year (L151); at the two fastest speeds pausing lands on the next January, since at those speeds the year's end is asked for half a year ahead, and at the three slower speeds it happens only in the last weeks of December. The speeds' names (Slow, Steady, Brisk, Fast, Fastest), a game starting at Steady, and a game opening on "January 1751" are the builder's choices.
- *Winter at every turn.* A clock stopped at a year's turn stands at January, so the opening card, every decision card and every moment an order is carried out sit over a winter island. The alternative is to ease to high summer wherever the bar shows a plain year, as the stills do.

## The look of 1900 to 2050: three questions

Written here on 20 November; asked since 19 November by the board of the look's three directions (an English industrial town grown on, dense and continental, tall and car-borne), whose pictures are with the night's reports on her machine, and described in the [backlog](../BACKLOG.md), the order of work, 3. She chooses by looking, and nothing that only one direction needs is built before she has answered. The houses as drawn are not changed by any of them.

- *Towers at true height:* do they read as a town, or as toys?
- *The old heart:* is the market place and church of the eighteenth century kept in the middle of the city, or let go?
- *The courtyard blocks:* do they feel cosy, or regimented?
