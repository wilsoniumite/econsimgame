## Hers

- **The core loop: what a family does from month to month, and why it is fun.** Twenty-five rows, nineteen faults, and none is to be taken up before she has said what the loop is. Land beats works, and the landlord comes first on every island (R169). Families own 57 per cent of the works' capital by 1900 (R170). A family that takes every default ends fourth of six, and why has not been traced (R67). The default first trade is usually estates (R172). Industrial rivals are feeble, under a million in 1900 against 17 to 25 million for the landed (R173). AI families never wind down, move their seat or hold, and their choices under the works life cycle were never examined (R175, R124, C103). Hostile buyouts remain round the seat (R176). A player who founds a works every year loses most of them (the founder bot founds 126 to 162 and has 26 to 39 standing, and ends below the idle player on three islands of four), and there is no order to extend a works (R114, C37). A family's works are not rationed by the year's room (R115, C31). Prospects shown as percentages steer the player to tiny works; a hint in money a year would steer better (R171). Every child turning fourteen pauses the game (R174). Under the price rule a family's first purse is worth half as much again (R82, C4). Families cannot buy town ground, and there is five times as much of it round a seat (R110, C115). Families and landlords have no Engel curve (R43). The player's place in the history is nearly always a succession, nine of thirteen kept items on seed 7 (R26, C75). The scripted players' ranks shifted on seed 7, one run each (R20). The politics half is not started, nor research and patents (R165, R166).
  - *The price of land.* `harvest::average_rent` adds back the crop the weather took, so land is priced about a tenth too high after a bad harvest and too low after a good one, and the only thing that reads it is the price the families and the player pay for land and the land in a family's worth. The mend is measured and is one line; the pass took it out for the core loop, to be decided with the years' purchase (sections 4.4 and 6; `docs/probes/tr-rent.md`, section 3). How far the correction is out hangs on `harvest.foreseen`.
  - *Exports and the price rule* (R78, C11). The rule's flow term cannot see exports, so an exporting town reads as a glut; mending it took the families' share of all money from 10 per cent to 66 in the probe. The pass leaves it with the core loop as the balance of the family game (section 6). Where: `docs/rules/prices.md`, "An open question for Stella".
  - *Word to the family given in the middle of a year takes effect after December,* before the books close. A seat moved in May gives the same game as one moved at the turn before; a branch's stance set in May is not in force until the year's end, where one set at a turn is in force for that year.
  - *A toast for a works she orders by hand* (R31, C73). Today it may get none and no place in the major feed; the checked table files it with the core loop. Where: one condition in the viewer's `main.rs`, toasting the player's own items at a weight of 0.6 or more.

## Needs a person at the window

No helper can judge these: each is how the game feels when it is played or watched at a real screen.

- *The smooth hand-over.* Its first stage landed; nobody has watched it at a screen. Whether the half second in which a new year's works stand among last year's trees is acceptable until a later stage cures it; and whether, at the fastest speed, a year shown smoothly about one time in two and a half is better than nearly every year with a third of a second's stop. F3 shows the frame strip. (`docs/probes/dx-smooth.md`, section 12)
- *The clock as it plays.* A pause late in a year moves the clock on to the next year (L151); at the two fastest speeds pausing lands on the next January, since at those speeds the year's end is asked for half a year ahead, and at the three slower speeds it happens only in the last weeks of December. The speeds' names (Slow, Steady, Brisk, Fast, Fastest), a game starting at Steady, and a game opening on "January 1751" are the builder's choices.
- *Winter at every turn.* A clock stopped at a year's turn stands at January, so the opening card, every decision card and every moment an order is carried out sit over a winter island. The alternative is to ease to high summer wherever the bar shows a plain year, as the stills do.
